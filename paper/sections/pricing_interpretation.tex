The weak- and strong-coupling scenarios distinguish an informative index from a noisy one. The global emissions surprise affects domestic repayment resources through the assumed damage wedge and the probability of a disaster. A coupon depending on that surprise can insure the relevant domestic states only to the extent that these links are strong. Ordinary growth shocks and logistics losses remain additional sources of uncertainty, and even a large emissions realization need not identify a default state for an individual issuer. An output-linked benchmark uses the variable determining capacity directly, whereas the emissions coupon conditions on a driver of only part of that variation.

Along an anticipated emissions path, the resource process can still deteriorate as chronic damages accumulate. That deterioration changes the level and possibly the distribution of fiscal capacity, so it matters for every debt instrument. It does not make the known component of a coupon adjustment a separate source of insurance. A complete dynamic climate application would need to distinguish the predictable path of resources, innovations around it, and the liabilities inherited from earlier financing decisions. The present experiments isolate the innovation channel and the stationary contract benchmark rather than conflating these three objects.

Additional pricing discounts introduce a further comparison. If lenders attach a novelty or ambiguity premium to a contingent claim, a reduction in expected default losses need not translate into a higher issue price at a common face. The evidence and models in \textcite{igan2021_premia_scdi} and \textcite{RochRoldan2023} motivate taking this possibility seriously. They do not supply a universal spread adjustment that can be inserted into the present calibration. Accordingly, the main tables quantify the risk-neutral mechanism, while the collateral appendix makes any extra pricing rule explicit. A welfare comparison would additionally need the value of expenditure smoothing and the costs of default to the sovereign.

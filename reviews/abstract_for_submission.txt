Can state-contingent sovereign debt expand borrowing capacity when climate risk thickens the left tail of output? This paper embeds contingent repayments in a fiscal-limit framework and adds acute disaster risk to chronic climate damages. In the stress illustration, a GDP-triggered clause with permanent relief raises one-period maximum sustainable borrowing by 11.2 percent; when issued repeatedly, the stationary gain is 47.3 percent. Gains are larger near the fiscal limit and depend on how closely relief identifies states that would otherwise default. A disaster-loss trigger delivers a smaller one-period gain of 3.3 percent because it misses adverse output states unrelated to a qualifying disaster. This mismatch is basis risk; the gap between the instruments' gains provides a design-specific measure. Separate advanced-economy and disaster-prone small-state illustrations, positive recovery, simulation uncertainty, and a stationary deferral extension show how the results depend on the fiscal environment and on whether relief is forgiven or carried forward. The illustrations are theoretical comparative statics, rather than estimates for individual issuers.
